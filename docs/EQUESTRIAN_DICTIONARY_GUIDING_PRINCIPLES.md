# Equestrian Dictionary guiding principles

## Mission

Make equestrian terminology understandable, accurate, and accessible to everyone. Knowledge should not require membership, prior riding experience, or familiarity with a particular discipline.

**Knowledge belongs to the community. Accuracy requires care. Education should welcome everyone.**

## Editorial practice

Write a concise plain-language definition for each meaning, followed by optional explanation, useful examples, related terminology, and relevant regional or discipline-specific usage. Explain unavoidable jargon. Prefer observable descriptions over judgments about a horse or rider.

Represent English, Western, driving, ranch, therapeutic, recreational, and competitive communities. Explain legitimate differences between training philosophies and traditions. Distinguish a common convention from an evidence-supported claim. Do not imply every interpretation has equal evidential support.

A term may have several meanings. Give each meaning a stable ID and distinct slug, clarify its context, and connect related meanings. Alternate spellings and synonyms point to the canonical meaning. Never silently change the meaning behind a published URL.

Treat subjective expressions such as “green” and “green broke” as descriptions needing context. Avoid presenting them as safety guarantees or standardized qualifications. Clinical, anatomical, and welfare claims require suitable editorial review.

## Community and privacy

Guests can browse, search, share, and suggest terms. Account creation is optional after a successful guest contribution. Registered contributors can suggest definitions, corrections, examples, synonyms, and regional usage.

Every submission enters a private review queue. Administrators retain final editorial control and publish reviewed wording through validated server operations. Submission text never becomes a public definition automatically.

Recognition is opt-in and unchecked by default. Publish only a separately supplied, approved public name when consent was given. Keep authentication IDs, email addresses, anti-abuse identifiers, review discussions, and private notes outside public documents. Withdrawing recognition requires an editorial revision and removal of the public name.

## Integrity and maintenance

Write definitions independently or record suitable licensing and permission. Resource text can identify candidate terms, but cannot be copied into definitions without rights. Starter content is editorial draft material requiring owner review before production release.

Review clarity, meaning, factual accuracy, safety, spelling, contextual ambiguity, relationships, attribution consent, and licensing before publication. Record revision number and publication time. Keep a private immutable revision snapshot for audit and recovery.

Use published definitions only for contextual matching. Prefer longer phrases, honor word boundaries, include approved synonyms, limit repetition, and disable automatic matching for ambiguous everyday words. Accessible popovers preserve the reading position and link to the full entry. Rendering definitions must work without AI requests.

## First implementation and release

The version-controlled starter collection is functions/dictionary_seed.json, also bundled by Flutter. Public entries, drafts, submissions, and editorial history have separate collections. See dictionary_implementation.md for the release checklist, anti-spam controls, and notification interfaces.
